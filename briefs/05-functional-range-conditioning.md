## Outline

1. **Intro (no heading).** Define FRC in the first two sentences: a system
   for joint health and mobility training developed by Dr. Andreo Spina

2. **## The Core Idea.** Training joints directly, building range and the
   strength to control it, the tissue-adaptation rationale. Link
   `/what-is-mobility`.
3. **## The Main Tools.** One short `###` each: CARs (link
   `/what-cars-are-actually-for`); PAILs and RAILs (link brief 04's slug once
   live); end-range lift-offs and isometrics (link
   `/isometrics-infinite-tension-infinite-potential`); assessment (link
   [Motive's Functional Range Assessment page](https://www.movewithpurpose.com/functional-range-assessment)).
4. **## The FRC Family.** FRC (the training system), Kinstretch (the group
   class format, link brief 06's slug once live), Functional Range Release
   (the manual therapy side), and Functional Range Assessment. Verify every
   name and what each covers against Functional Anatomy Seminars' site.
5. **## What an FRC Session Looks Like.** A plain description of a session at
   Motive: assessment, CARs, targeted end-range work, then using it in
   training. Keep it general; no invented client details.
6. **## Who It's For.** Lifters with stubborn restrictions, people coming back
   from injury (with a clinician), older adults wanting joint longevity, and
   coaches. Who might not need it.
7. **## Where the Claims Outrun the Evidence.** Honest: limited direct
   research on FRC as a system; the principles (resistance training improves
   range, isometrics build strength at specific angles) have better support.
   Link `/what-cars-are-actually-for` where the site made this argument.
8. **## How to Try It.** Find an FRC-certified coach (verify how the official
