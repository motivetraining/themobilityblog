## Search Intent

A comparison question with a practical decision behind it: which one do I do,
and when. Most readers have heard "dynamic before, static after" and want it
confirmed or explained. Give the short answer in the first two sentences.

## Angle

The common rule is roughly right about warm-ups and wrong about what it
implies. The comparison only makes sense once you name the goal:

- Getting ready for today's session: dynamic work and practice of the
  movement itself.
- Changing range over weeks: static (and PNF) stretching has the better
  evidence, at a dose most people never reach.
- Being able to use that range: neither is enough, which is where loading
  and end-range isometrics come in.

The second point is the one most articles on the first page miss, and the
site has already done the reading for it.

## Outline

1. **Intro (no heading).** Scene: the warm-up area before a run or a lift,
   one person swinging a leg and another holding a hamstring stretch, each
   sure the other is doing it wrong. Then the direct answer.
2. **## What Is the Difference Between Static and Dynamic Stretching?**
   One-sentence definitions of each, then where ballistic stretching and PNF
   sit so the terms are clear.
3. **## What Static Stretching Does.** Short-term: range goes up; long holds
   can briefly reduce force output (state the hold durations the research
   tested, and that short holds inside a full warm-up show little or no
   effect). Long-term: range improves with enough weekly volume.
4. **## What Dynamic Stretching Does.** Raises temperature and rehearses the
   movement; short-term range and performance effects. Be straight that its
   evidence for building lasting range is weaker.
5. **## Is Dynamic Stretching Better Than Static?** Better for what. Lay the
   three goals from the Angle section side by side. A small table is fine
   here (goal, better tool, why).
6. **## Static and Dynamic Stretching Exercises.** Two or three paired
   examples for the same area (hamstrings, hip flexors, calves): the static
   version, the dynamic version, a dose for each.
7. **## Where the Evidence Gets Messy.** Study populations, short
   interventions, performance tests that don't look like real training,
   injury-prevention claims that outrun the data.
8. **## What Neither One Does.** Range you can't control is range you can't
   use. Link the flexibility-versus-mobility post and the why-it-doesn't-stick
   post. One short paragraph, then the CTA.
9. **Short practical wrap-up:** what to do before training, after, and on
   its own.
10. **## References**

## Must Include

- The direct answer in the first two sentences.
- Hold durations and study details stated with their source in the same
  sentence.
- A safety line: when stretching of either kind should stop (sharp or
  pinching pain, numbness or tingling, hypermobile joints).
