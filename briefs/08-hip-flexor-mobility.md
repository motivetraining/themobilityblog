## Search Intent

People who feel "tight" at the front of the hip, often from sitting or
running, who have stretched without lasting change and want exercises.

## Angle

Hip flexors have two jobs at two ends of the range: they need length to let
the hip extend, and strength to lift the thigh high into flexion. Most people
only stretch them, but "tight" hip flexors are often weak at short length, and
the nervous system guards a muscle it can't control. The post trains both
ends.

## Outline

1. **Intro (no heading).** Scene: years of couch stretches, and the front of
   the hip still feels tight on every run.
2. **## Test Both Ends.** Length: a modified Thomas test (verify the setup
   and what it does and doesn't tell you). Strength at short length: seated
   or standing active hip flexion, lifting the knee above 90 degrees without
   leaning back, and holding it. Compare the active lift to how high the knee
   goes when pulled up by hand.
3. **## Why Stretching Alone Stalls.** Two paragraphs, then link
   `/why-mobility-doesnt-stick` for load, intent, and frequency.
4. **## Hip Flexor Mobility Exercises for Length.** Brief: the kneeling
   stretch done with a posterior pelvic tilt (point to
   `/why-mobility-doesnt-stick` for its PAILs/RAILs version rather than
   repeating it), plus one different drill such as a rear-foot-elevated split
   squat held at the bottom, or a long-lever reverse lunge.
5. **## Hip Flexor Mobility Exercises for Strength.** The new material.
   `###` each with dose: seated knee lift-offs, standing knee raise
   end-range isometric holds, a psoas march (banded), hanging or supported
   knee raises with a slow lower.
6. **## A 10-Minute Routine.** Combine both ends, three to four times a week.
7. **## When It's Not the Hip Flexors.** Pinching at the front of the hip
   (possible impingement; link `/hip-internal-rotation-the-range-most-people-lose`'s
   caveat), groin pain, low back pain that changes with the stretch. Refer
   out.
