## Outline

1. **Intro (no heading).** One-sentence definition first, then the current
   longer definition. Then one short paragraph on why the word is so muddy in
   fitness (it gets used for stretching, warm-ups, and rehab).
2. **## Mobility vs. Flexibility.** Passive range vs actively controlled range.
   The "active-passive gap" concept: the difference between how far a joint
   can be moved and how far you can move it yourself. Link
   `/flexibility-is-range-mobility-is-what-you-can-do-with-it`.
3. **## What Mobility Is Made Of.** Three components, a short paragraph each:
   available range (tissue and joint capsule), strength at that range, and
   control (the nervous system's willingness to go there). Why missing any one
   shows up as "tightness".
4. **## How to Tell If You Have a Mobility Problem.** Two simple self-checks
   that need no equipment: a CAR for the joint (link
   `/what-cars-are-actually-for`), and the active vs passive comparison (for
   example, how high you can lift a straight leg yourself vs how high it goes
   when pulled). Signs: range that disappears under load, compensation
   elsewhere, a position you can only reach passively.
5. **## How Mobility Is Trained.** The progression the site teaches, one short
   paragraph each with a link: CARs to maintain and assess; PAILs and RAILs
   (link brief 04's slug once live, Motive's guide until then); end-range
   isometrics (link `/isometrics-infinite-tension-infinite-potential`); then
   using the range under load in real training (link brief 09's slug once
   live). Link `/why-mobility-doesnt-stick` for load, intent, frequency.
6. **## How Often, and How Long Before It Changes.** Practical dose: short,
   frequent sessions. Be honest that timelines vary and that research on
   specific methods is limited. Cite resistance-training-for-ROM evidence.
7. **## Who Needs Mobility Training.** Lifters, runners, desk workers, older
   adults (link brief 07's slug once live), people coming back from injury
   (with a clinician). Mention that hypermobile people need control more than
   range, linking the guest post.
8. **## Common Myths.** Three short ones: "mobility is just stretching",
   "mobility is a warm-up" (link `/mobility-isnt-a-warm-up-its-the-work`),
   "more range is always better".
9. **## Where to Go Next.** A short list of the site's joint-specific posts
   (hip IR, ankle, and the new 90/90, shoulder, thoracic, wrist posts as they
   publish). This is the hub section; keep it current.
