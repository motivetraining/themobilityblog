## Outline

1. **Intro (no heading).** Spell out both acronyms in the first 100 words:
   Progressive Angular Isometric Loading and Regressive Angular Isometric
   Loading, from Functional Range Conditioning. One sentence on what they're
   for.
2. **## What PAILs and RAILs Actually Do.** PAILs: contracting the tissue
   being stretched, at length. RAILs: contracting the opposite side to pull
   deeper into the range. Why the pairing matters.
3. **## How a Set Works.** The general structure: settle into a passive
   stretch, then the PAILs contraction ramped gradually, then the RAILs
   contraction, then hold the new range. Verify the timings against official
   FRC material or Motive's guide; if they can't be confirmed, describe the
   structure without exact seconds. Link Motive's guide for the full protocol.
4. **## PAILs and RAILs Exercises.** Two or three worked examples: 90/90 hip
   (link brief 01's slug once live), hamstring, and shoulder external
   rotation. Short, so the post doesn't duplicate Motive's guide.
5. **## When to Use Them, and When Not To.** Good fits: building new range,
   after CARs reveal a limit. Poor fits: right before heavy lifting (verify any
   claim about strength drops after hard isometrics before stating it),
   painful joints, hypermobile people chasing more range (link
   `/when-stretching-makes-pain-worse-how-to-spot-the-red-flags`).
6. **## Common Mistakes.** Going to max effort immediately instead of ramping;
   doing them every day at high intensity; skipping the RAILs; holding the
   breath; doing them without then using the range.
7. **## What the Research Says.** Honest: very little research tests PAILs and
   RAILs by name. Related evidence: contract-relax and PNF stretching, long-
   length isometrics, and resistance training improving range of motion. Say
   clearly that the method is built on reasonable principles and coaching
   experience more than direct trials.
8. **## Where They Fit in a Program.** CARs, then PAILs and RAILs, then
   loaded use of the range. Link `/what-cars-are-actually-for` and
   `/why-mobility-doesnt-stick`.
