## Search Intent

Practical with a performance tilt: lifters with a poor overhead position,
golfers and throwers who need rotation, and desk workers with a stiff upper
back. Some searchers are in pain; the post should help them and tell them
when it's not a mobility problem.

## Angle

The thoracic spine is built to rotate and extend, and when it doesn't, the
neck, shoulders, and low back pick up the slack. Foam rolling and passive
twists feel good but rarely hold. The post pairs each passive drill with an
active one that makes the range usable.

## Outline

1. **Intro (no heading).** Scene: an overhead press or a golf swing where the
   low back does the rotating or arching the upper back should be doing.
2. **## What the Thoracic Spine Is Supposed to Do.** Short anatomy: twelve
   vertebrae, ribs attached, built for more rotation than the lumbar spine.
   Cite a source for the rotation comparison.
3. **## Test Your Rotation and Extension.** Seated rotation test (arms
   crossed, hips fixed, compare sides); an extension check (back to wall,
   overhead reach, or quadruped extension). What compensation looks like.
4. **## Thoracic Rotation Exercises.** `###` per drill with dose: open book
   with an active end-range hold; quadruped rotation with hand behind head;
   half-kneeling rotation against a wall or with a dowel; PAILs and RAILs in
   side-lying rotation (link brief 04's slug once live).
5. **## Thoracic Extension Exercises.** Foam roller extension, done properly
   (moving the roller segment by segment, ribs down, no lumbar arch), plus why
   it stops short on its own; segmental cat-cow or spinal CARs (link
   `/what-cars-are-actually-for`); prone extension lift-offs.
6. **## Making It Stick.** Loaded rotation and extension in real training:
   half-kneeling cable or landmine rotation, overhead carries. Frequency. Link
   `/why-mobility-doesnt-stick`.
7. **## When Upper Back Pain Isn't a Mobility Problem.** Pain that's
   constant, worse at night, or comes with other symptoms such as unexplained
   weight loss or numbness is a reason to see a doctor, not to stretch.
   Keep it calm and factual.
