## Search Intent

Lifters who've been told they're "too stiff" and people who think lifting
makes you less flexible. Both want to know if strength training helps
mobility and how to set it up.

## Angle

This is the site's thesis with receipts: strength at length is mobility. The
research on resistance training and range of motion is better than most
people think, and the details (full range, long-length emphasis, slow
eccentrics) decide whether a lift builds range or just strength in the middle.

## Outline

1. **Intro (no heading).** The old idea that lifting makes you "muscle-bound",
   and what the research actually says, in two sentences.
2. **## What the Research Shows.** Alizadeh 2023 and Afonso 2021 (verify
   both). State the findings exactly. Caveats: who was studied, how long, which
   joints.
3. **## Why Lifting Builds Range.** Mechanisms, carefully hedged: tolerance to
   stretch, strength at long lengths, and possible structural changes from
   eccentric training at length (cite or say it's proposed).
4. **## How to Choose Lifts That Build Range.** Principles: full range of
   motion, emphasis on the stretched position, slow eccentrics, pauses at
   length, and loading the range you're building rather than only the range
   you have.
5. **## Strength and Mobility Exercises.** `###` each with dose: Romanian
   deadlift (hamstrings at length), rear-foot-elevated split squat (hip
   flexors and quads), Cossack squat (adductors), deficit push-up or dumbbell
   fly (pecs and shoulder), and one loaded rotation (link brief 03). Verify any
   claims about specific exercises.
6. **## A Strength and Mobility Workout.** One sample session, about 45
   minutes, with sets and reps.
7. **## Where Stretching and End-Range Work Still Fit.** Lifting won't reach
   every range (the end of hip internal rotation, for example). That's where
   CARs, PAILs and RAILs, and isometrics come in. Link
   `/hip-internal-rotation-the-range-most-people-lose`, brief 04,
