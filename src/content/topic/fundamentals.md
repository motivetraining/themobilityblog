Most of what gets argued on this site comes back to one distinction in mobility training: the range you can get into versus the range you can control. Flexibility is passive range, the position gravity, a strap, or a partner can put you in. Mobility is the range a joint can reach and hold on its own, with enough control that you can use it. Both have a place, but the second is what carries over into lifting, sport, and the rest of your day. Mobility also behaves like any other capacity you train: it needs load, intent, and frequency to build, and it fades when you stop maintaining it.

If you're new to this, start with [What Is Mobility? A Coach's Definition, and How to Train It](/what-is-mobility). It sets up the vocabulary the rest of the site uses and walks through how to test a joint before you train it. If you already do mobility work but treat it as a few quick drills before the real session, read [Mobility Isn't a Warm-Up. It's the Work.](/mobility-isnt-a-warm-up-its-the-work); it makes the case for giving joint control the same intent and progression you'd give a lift.

From here, the [Stretching](/topics/stretching) posts get into how much work it takes to change range in the first place, and where chasing more of it is the wrong goal.
